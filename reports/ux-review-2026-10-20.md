# EduPlanet website review: UX, enquiries, applications and consistency

Reviewed 20 October 2026. Recommendations for revision and approval; no website changes were made during this review.

## Overall assessment

The current design is professional and recognisably EduPlanet. The navy and blue palette, clear typography, genuine school photographs, Christian identity and phase pages form a strong foundation. Keep these.

The largest opportunities are completing the parent journey, making practical answers easier to find, and replacing repeated general statements with evidence relevant to each phase. The website is not excessively cluttered visually. It does become long and repetitive, especially on a phone. More sections or more photographs everywhere would not solve that.

**Recommended direction:** a concise school overview on Home, meaningful detail on each phase page, and one dependable set of fees, application and appointment actions across the site.

## Scope and limits

- Reviewed the main content and shared components of all 10 current pages, including the four phase pages.
- Inspected desktop layouts and narrow mobile layouts, including approximately 360 px and 433 px browser widths for key journeys. Checked mobile navigation, phase submenu, grade selection, fee inclusions, scholarship form and dialog dismissal.
- Inspected the first page of the public Google application form. No information was entered or submitted. Its second page and submission confirmation were not tested.
- Compared the public Admissions page with the latest local version. Reused the earlier legacy-link audit for old incoming URLs.
- This is an evidence-based usability review, not a measured conversion-rate study, full accessibility certification or performance benchmark. No analytics account, completed applications, booking records or inbox delivery evidence was available.

## 2. Make the parent journey predictable

Support three intentions without making parents follow a mandatory website sequence:

1. **I am deciding whether this school fits my child:** choose a phase, understand the support, view fees, request an appointment.
2. **I am ready to apply:** Apply now → the Google application form directly.
3. **I am interested in sponsorship:** fees → optional scholarship-interest action, with the conditions visible.

Keep fees ungated. The contact-details popup should remain removed. Keep the full on-page Admissions benefits section **and** the shorter expandable summary inside the fee popup, as requested. These serve different contexts; use the same source wording for overlapping benefits.

### One consistent set of actions

| Action | Meaning everywhere | Presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Apply now** | Open the approved Google application form directly | Primary action for parents ready to proceed; use the same wording and destination throughout. |
| **View fees** | Open the existing grade-based fee popup on the current page | Make available from Home and shared navigation/footer, as well as phase and Admissions pages. |
| **Make an appointment** | Open a functioning booking option | Until booking is connected, use **Request an appointment** with a direct contact route. |
| **Explore this phase** | Open the relevant phase page | Use phase name and grade range together nearby. |
| **Register scholarship interest** | Open the separate interest form | Clearly secondary to the ordinary application action; conditions remain adjacent. |

Do not put all five actions into every section. A hero and final action block are sufficient on most pages. Use contextual text links between them.

## 4. Reduce repetition without losing the benefits

Give each benefit a main home, then use short reminders elsewhere:

| Message | Full explanation belongs on | Reminder elsewhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Christian vision, mission and values | About | One sentence on Home; a phase-specific example on phase pages. |
| Teaching, small classes and academic support | Academics | Three proof points on Home; how the support works for each age group on phase pages. |
| Fees, inclusions and enrolment process | Admissions and shared fee popup | View fees / Apply now actions elsewhere. |
| Daily participation, sport, camps and service | School Life | A photo or short example on the relevant phase page. |
| Hours, location and how to reach the team | Contact | A consistent footer and contextual appointment link. |

The four phase pages currently repeat headings such as “Support that grows with your child”, “Participation, practice and personal growth”, “A clear foundation for the next stage” and “A journey with continuity”. Their shared layout is useful; their section content should answer different parent questions.

Suggested phase-page structure: concise introduction and relevant image → three phase-specific benefits → how learning/support work → subjects → practical parent questions → fees/application/appointment actions. Combine sections when they repeat the same point.

## 5. Fees, forms and mobile details

### Fee popup

- Keep the grade dropdown, prominent monthly figure and expandable inclusions. The current narrow layout fits without horizontal overflow in the tested widths.
- At 360 px, Apply now falls below the initially visible part of the popup. Expanding benefits adds substantial scrolling. Consider a compact sticky action area or a shorter initial summary so the next action remains easy to reach. Keep the close control accessible while scrolling; avoid adding another full-screen overlay.
- Keep registration visibly separate from monthly tuition. Confirm and explain the payment schedule, advance-payment conditions and additional compulsory costs; do not infer missing terms from the discount table.
- Preserve the chosen grade when closing/reopening during the same visit. Currently it resets each time. A phase page can suggest the relevant grade range, but should not silently choose a specific grade that the parent has not selected.
- Show the same scope and conditions for the same benefit in the page and popup. Do not imply that team kits apply to every learner when the supplied wording specifies high-school sports teams.
- Keep scholarship interest secondary. The current long scholarship button takes considerable mobile space; use a short action label with its explanatory lines beside/below it.

### Google application form

The first page contains approximately 16 questions, including **11 required questions**, before Next. It combines application information with research questions about budgets, priorities and reasons for changing schools.

Recommendations:

1. Remove RR and use a clear current application title. “Fees & Application Form” is confusing after parents have already viewed fees on the website.
2. Begin with grade, intended start and core parent/learner information. Review which fields are essential at this stage; move optional marketing questions later or remove them where they will not be used.
3. Avoid requiring the same number twice for Phone and WhatsApp. Use an optional alternative WhatsApp number or explain that it can be left blank when the same, if the form design permits.
4. Replace “Bottom (Needs special attention)” with respectful, support-focused wording. For Grade R, check whether comparison with a school class is applicable at all. Ask for useful support information rather than a potentially discouraging label.
5. Correct “Budget contraints”; align capitalisation and restrained tone with the website. Interest options such as music/drama must read as parent preferences, not evidence that the school offers those programmes.
6. Before the website handover, explain what parents need ready. Only add a completion-time estimate after someone has tested it. Make clear whether sign-in is optional; the first page currently offers Google sign-in to save progress rather than requiring it.
7. Review page two and the confirmation with the school owner: explain next steps and how to request an appointment. Do not imply a successful application click is a completed application or admission.

### Scholarship interest

The local form opens correctly and carries over the selected grade. Escape returns to the fee popup and the scholarship action. It collects name, phone, email and grade rather than an academic assessment.

Activation has been confirmed by the owner, but actual inbox delivery has not been independently verified. Complete one owner-approved end-to-end delivery test before relying on it, and retain a direct contact fallback for errors. Use success wording that acknowledges receipt of interest without implying eligibility, funding or admission. No submission was made in this review.

## 6. Visual and editorial consistency

- **Phase names:** use Foundation Phase · Grades R–3; Intermediate Phase · Grades 4–6; Senior Phase · Grades 7–9; and one agreed name for Grades 10–12, such as FET Phase · Grades 10–12. “Matric Years”, “FET” and grade-only titles currently vary. Agree the public-facing name before changing it.
- **Buttons:** use sentence case consistently: Apply now, View fees, Make an appointment. The current header/body mix Apply Now / Apply now and several visit phrases.
- **Footer:** use the same useful application, fees and appointment links everywhere. Admissions currently has a different Apply column from the other nine pages.
- **Taglines:** choose one primary strapline for prominent brand sign-offs. “Belong. Grow. Make a Difference.” and “Gratitude • Excellence • Service” can coexist as purpose and values, but should have distinct, consistent roles.
- **Typography:** maintain the strong headings, but reduce lengthy line-wrapping on narrow phones. The Senior hero occupies much of the first screen before the image or substantive detail appears.
- **Captions:** avoid generic photo labels such as “Teacher reading with young learner” when the visible caption could explain a meaningful activity accurately. Keep alt text factual and useful; do not stuff it with promotional keywords.
- **Practical language:** a link labelled “Admissions and Daily Arrangements” currently lands at the benefits section. The anchor exists, but its destination does not answer detailed questions about daily times or arrangements. Match the label to the content or restore that practical information.

## 7. Photographs, reels and proof

Use visual evidence selectively, rather than adding an image to every text section.

- **Home:** retain a strong school overview. Consider a short learner/teacher moment that demonstrates personal attention rather than relying mainly on buildings. Keep headline and actions readable.
- **Foundation:** warm teacher interaction, practical learning and a reassuring daily routine.
- **Intermediate:** classroom independence, collaborative learning and teacher feedback.
- **Senior:** older learners, mentoring, subject exploration and participation beyond computers.
- **FET:** senior academic work, subject teaching, revision or preparation for life beyond school.
- **About / School Life:** real examples of service, pastoral development, camps and school community, when approved material is available.

One relevant, captioned, click-to-play reel on an appropriate page is a useful experiment. Avoid multiple autoplay reels or carousels competing with the main action. Use approved footage and a poster image; supply captions for spoken content.

The claims that most need supporting evidence are improved academic performance, experienced/qualified teachers and externally verified academic standards. Provide actual results with year/cohort context, approved staff credentials, and the named examination arrangement once confirmed. Parent quotes should be current, approved and specific. Do not invent results, testimonials or guarantees.

## 8. Accessibility, speed and search discovery

### Existing strengths

The site already has a skip link, descriptive image alternatives, visible-focus styling, mobile menu controls, native expandable sections and native dialogs. Below-the-fold photos are lazy-loaded. The hero video already falls back to a poster for reduced-motion/data-saver preferences and pauses when offscreen. These are worth retaining.

The fee dialog’s Escape dismissal and focus return worked in the tested journey, including returning from scholarship interest to the fees dialog. This is not a complete keyboard/screen-reader audit. Preserve these behaviours when changing the popup. [W3C modal pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/).

### Improvements

- Add a visible pause/play control for the looping hero video. Reduced-motion support is useful but does not replace a pause control for all users when motion runs alongside reading content. [W3C Pause, Stop, Hide guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html).
- Keep important phone controls comfortably sized and spaced. The mobile menu and phase arrow measured 44 px high in the tested state; phone/WhatsApp controls measured 40 px. Larger targets can improve ease of use, but do not describe 44 px as the WCAG AA minimum: the target-size rule has a 24 px minimum with exceptions. [W3C target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).
- Test mobile keyboard access, visible focus, zoom, contrast and long-dialog scrolling on an actual phone before release. The responsive review did not reveal horizontal document overflow at the tested widths.
- The homepage video is about 2.5 MB in WebM, with an approximately 3.7 MB MP4 fallback and `preload="auto"`. Measure whether it delays the parent’s first useful screen on mobile connections; consider a poster-first mobile experience if it does. Do not download/play more promotional video automatically.
- Consider responsive image sources and modern formats. Image markup lacks intrinsic width/height in many places, although CSS already reserves aspect ratios in several layouts; missing attributes alone do not prove layout instability.
- Measure real Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift rather than inventing a speed score. [Core Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).
- After fixing production indexing, add distinct page descriptions, canonical URLs and a current sitemap. All local pages currently reuse the same generic description and lack canonical tags.
- Implement the relevant old-address redirects from the previous audit, prioritising admissions, fees, contact and phase pages. The earlier check confirmed 24 old school addresses returning 404. Use the closest relevant destination, not a blanket homepage redirect. See [legacy-link audit](link-audit-2026-10-20.md) and [redirect mapping](legacy-redirect-recommendations-2026-10-20.csv). That older report’s application/fee-gate recommendations have been superseded by the current direct-application, ungated-fees decisions.

## 9. Measure useful outcomes

No site analytics instrumentation was found in the reviewed source. Establish a baseline before drawing conclusions about conversion rates.

Track a small set of actions without including personal form data:

- phase-page visits and phase selected;
- fee popup opened and grade selected;
- application link clicked, including originating page/phase;
- appointment, WhatsApp and phone actions;
- scholarship-interest form successfully submitted, separately from ordinary applications.

Then compare with actual completed Google Form applications, attended appointments, offers and accepted places from the school's records. An application-link click is not a completed application; a booking-link click is not an attended visit. Google Form completion requires its own response evidence or an agreed integration, rather than being inferred from website clicks.

Useful measures: completed applications per relevant site visit, fee-view-to-application-click rate, appointment attendance and eventual accepted places. Segment by phase and device if volumes support it. For small volumes, use a few structured parent usability sessions instead of treating noisy A/B percentages as reliable.

## Recommended order of work

1. **Complete and verify the public actions:** application link, appointment route, PDF, current Google Form choices, scholarship delivery and production indexing.
2. **Make fees easy to find and costs clear:** shared View fees access, included/optional distinction, predictable popup actions.
3. **Tighten content and consistency:** phase names, buttons, footer, mobile headings and duplicated sections; retain the official vision/mission and approved benefits.
4. **Add proof and targeted media:** verified results, approved parent experiences and phase-appropriate imagery or a single relevant reel.
5. **Measure and refine:** collect a baseline and test changes against completed applications and appointments, not clicks alone.

The recommended next revision is a focused improvement to the existing design, with page-by-page review before publication. It does not require a new admissions system or another complete website variant.
